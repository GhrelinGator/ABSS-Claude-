## Known risks

- **Predation on lone sleepers is heavy** (D-052). Blank founders lose up to 7 in 100 to a big cat in their first weeks on some seeds (none on others): a cat finds lone, fireless sleepers easier than deer. It is the world, not a calibration target, but it is a strong pressure on 3.4–3.7's learners; Phase 3's exit (a population of solo learners persisting 5 generations) should report deaths by animals beside the viability tuning, and no viability tune may touch the predators without the nudge protocol.
- **Hunting by hand is close to impossible** (D-052): deer run after one blow and an aurochs needs many strikers. Meat comes from scavenging (carcasses of predators' kills, which fed predators give up to persons). Projectiles and traps are not built; a hoped-for phenomenon that needs hunting will find this floor.

- **Infancy under the compressed year** (D-046, Q11). Nursed infants need about 1.5 times a real infant's energy at the same mass; a newborn given only solid food outlives the weeks before it can digest any; mothers with no surplus starve after two or three children, since fertility is gated by energy balance and not by fat stores. Phase 4's preregistrations must name it unless the owner changes the clock.
- **Reproduction numbers, partly checked** (D-046, D-048). 2.8 read Butte & King (2005), FAO's lactation chapter and Nybo Andersen et al. (2000): pregnancy's cost, its basal rise, fat gained, milk's energy, efficiency and output, and loss at 20–24 and 45 all match what 2.6 used. Still from memory: the energy-balance gate (Valeggia & Ellison 2009), fecundability, the age pattern after 30, birth bleeding. Fertility has no suckling term: a mother fed for all she gives conceives again within about a year and a half.

- **Old age may be counted twice once acts exist** (D-045). The whole senescent term of Gurven & Kaplan's fit is death by old age now; once Phase 3's acts read capacity and strength (which age lowers), old bodies will also die more of predation, injury and hunger. 2.8 should compare age-rising deaths with the fit and lower the old-age hazard if they are counted twice. Koster et al.'s skill by age is a target for foraging returns then, not for any one part.
- **Growth under the compressed year** (D-045). A newborn nearly triples its frame in 48 real days; a child with no food to spare doesn't grow at all, and one held at the edge of its fat (fed exactly what it spends) drifts slowly toward no fat at all without starving, since starvation is counted by lean mass. 2.6 and 2.8 should look at both.
- **Fitness and skill numbers are mostly from memory** (D-043, `RESEARCH.md`). Banister's constants, the strain hazard and the acute:chronic risk factor (Gabbett 2016 could not be read), the muscle target and its time constants, and skill's disuse are estimates; Koster et al. (2020) was read and the practice curve matches it. The acute:chronic ratio is itself contested as a predictor. 2.8 should check them.
- **Animals and agents do not meet yet** (D-051): no hunting, no flight from agents, no predators attacking agents, until 3.3b. Until then fear has only fire to learn from.
- **Blank minds die of thirst in two to three weeks** (D-051, §4.9.8). Expected for a random policy; a learner that cannot find water again before 3.4's place memory would too.
- **Founders' conditioning** (D-043, D-051): picked in 3.3 as a forager's load (8 load-hours a day), a body choice; a Fable look may weigh it (listed above).
- **Injury and infection numbers are mostly from memory** (D-042, `RESEARCH.md`). Wound-infection deaths (10.8% fed, 25.4% thin) follow from an estimated virulence spread and immune rise, against a target that is itself an estimate; doses per kg of water and meat at a "full bloom" were set here. Phase 3 will make them matter: which water and meat make agents ill is one of the real hidden causes agents could learn. 2.8 should check them first.
- **Runs can't chain themselves** (found 2026-11-19, session 22). Asked to create the next one-shot run, the scheduling tool refused: inside a scheduled run, creating, changing or deleting a scheduled task needs the owner's approval, given in a turn that answers a message from him. That likely explains why the 1.12, 1.13 and 2.1 runs didn't chain either (D-040). The 2.3 run (session 23) met the same refusal. Until that changes, the hourly wake-up is the chain: a milestone starts within about an hour of the last one merging.
- **Cold stress on naked bodies** (D-041). In this climate a naked sleeper shivers away about 1,100 kcal a day at 20 °C, and on a clear winter night in the open shivers at its maximum and still cools. That is the physics of an unclothed body in a temperate climate, and Phase 3's founders will meet it. Phase 3.7's viability work will have to look at it (the season founders start in, the climate setting) without nudging behaviour. Also, 2.1's scripted Hadza day at 20–32 °C now costs about 3,260 kcal with the cold nights counted.
- **Heat and water numbers are mostly from memory** (`RESEARCH.md`). 2.8 confirmed through Greenleaf (1973) and Allen (1956) that Adolph put fatal deficits at 15–25% of body mass (§3.9 uses 15%) and survival at about 10 days resting in shade below 21 °C; the table by temperature (3 days at 43 °C) is still unverified, and so are the heat-exchange constants.
- **Energy spending, corrected in 2.8** (T-014, D-048). The thermic effect was counted twice; scripted Hadza-like days now cost 2,541 and 1,962 kcal. §9's absolute targets and the Hadza body masses are still from memory (Pontzer et al.'s Table 1 could not be read); as PAL over Henry's BMR the men's scripted day is light (1.87 against 2.26 ± 0.48). Schofield charges fat at muscle's rate (D-048).
- **No ceiling on sustained spending but the gut** (D-048). A random stand-in policy spent 4,500–4,800 kcal a day and starved everyone; real people sustain about 2.5 × the basal rate at most (from memory). Phase 3's acts must read capacity (§3.11), or minds free to work flat out will starve the same way.
- **No adaptive thermogenesis** (D-039). Real starving bodies slow their metabolism more than lost tissue explains; these don't, so they starve somewhat sooner (43 and 57 days for the lean test bodies).
- **Compute budget, now tight** (D-016, D-049, D-050, D-051). Measured in 3.3 with real acts: the whole pipeline costs 5.2–7.5 µs per agent-tick before the kernel (~0.5) and before 3.4's learning (the prototype's ~1–1.2 at this decision rate), against 6.94. The mind's look (~6 µs) and exposure of walking bodies (~0.8 µs per agent-tick) are the largest parts. 3.4 must measure again; if it cannot fit, fewer agents or slower generations is the owner's call. Measured in 3.2: perception costs 0.6–0.9 µs per agent per tick, and exposure got 0.35 cheaper, so about 3.7 µs per agent-tick is left for minds at 500 agents, against the 1–1.2 the 3.1 prototype needs if acts last (about 0.25 decisions per awake agent-tick, `DESIGN.md` §4.7.12). 3.3–3.4 must measure again with real acts; if they can't keep to it, the fallback is fewer agents or slower generations, a conversation with the owner, not a cheaper mind slipped in.
- **Perception's numbers are estimates** (D-050, `DESIGN.md` §4.8): every range, the light bands and factors, the concealment rule, the 150 m for telling who a person is. None is tuned toward anything; Phase 4 should look again at the person ranges, which decide how much agents can know about each other.
- **Portable math is in place** (D-014, D-018), and measurement showed it was needed: two engines' built-in `exp` disagreed on 10% of inputs. What remains a risk is discipline: a system that needs a function `dmath.ts` lacks (`atan2`, say) has to add it properly, with tests and a digest, not reach for the built-in. The guardrail test blocks the built-ins.
- **Founder viability.** Blank-slate founders may die before learning enough. Viability tuning is allowed (logged), but if it takes heavy tuning, the floor may need rethinking. That's a conversation with the owner, not a quiet fix.
- **Floor 1 expressiveness** (`DESIGN.md` §6.7). The legitimation chain may be inexpressible with unordered token bags.
- **Claude's drift** (`GUARDRAILS.md`). The guardrail tests catch words, imports, and bookkeeping. They can't judge whether an alibi is true or whether a number was tuned toward an outcome.
- **Long-run plant balance** (for 1.10). In 20 seeds × 100 years on small maps, no species died out and none grew without bound, but greens and reeds fell to about a fifth of their starting cover in runs with large year-to-year swings: riverbank soil dried in a drought like any other. Since 1.12 a water table keeps stream banks moist (`DESIGN.md` §2.11). Animals add grazing pressure on top: on open land, deer browse greens down to 28–66% of their start.
- **Fire regime, accepted as a known limit** (D-037, D-038). In the last exit check open country burns 1.18% of its land a compressed year (0.58–2.45% by world) and hemmed valleys 0.18%; a dry autumn can burn most of an open world (worst year 102%). E3 must name fire as a difference between open and walled land.
- **Hemmed seed 5's deer** (D-038). Still falling at year 100 (1.39 per km² at year 70, 0.49 at year 100) under wolves and big cats held up by hares and aurochs; hunger deaths are low. A predator pit, not a fault found, but that world's persistence past 100 compressed years is not shown. Long runs in walled valleys (Phase 3 onward) may lose deer there.
- **Loops in simple animal behaviour** (T-009). Open seed 6 lost hundreds of deer a year for decades to a loop between fleeing and walking to water. The degenerate case that caused it is fixed; the map edge still stops a flight dead and animals don't die of thirst, so a shorter loop at the edge is possible in principle. `experiments/P1-open-seed-6/diagnose.ts` finds starving groups and where they stand.
- **Fire regime** (for 1.10). Fires are rare and mostly small in the default climate, which fits humid temperate land, but in a severe drought on an open plain one fire can burn nearly the whole map. Limiters real landscapes have and this model lacks: moist riverbanks (no water table), patchy fuel inside a cell, and spotting (which would make fires bigger, not smaller). Eleven rounds of correction are in T-005; the next look should be by fresh eyes.
- **Rock shelters follow the circumscription knob** (for E3, D-029). Cliffs come with walls, so a walled valley has about 34 rock shelters per 100 km² and open country about 1.5. That is real geography, but E3's preregistration has to account for it.
- **After the ecology review (D-032):** open-country deer settle at 11–18 per km², above field densities with wolves (3–6 per km² in Białowieża); greens are browsed to a quarter to two-fifths of their start on some open seeds; predators in walled valleys number a dozen or fewer, so long runs can lose them by chance.
- **Animal balance** (from 1.5, reviewed in 1.10). Predators die out on a small, hemmed-in map with large climate swings; deer gather in tight clusters on the best browse; most animal numbers are estimates (`RESEARCH.md`). Eleven rounds of correction went into the current balance (T-004), so it should be reviewed by fresh eyes, not just extended.
- **Root refilling rates are estimates** (T-003). Real tuber turnover hasn't been looked up yet; Phase 3's foraging-return calibration will test it.
- **Claude can't watch.** I read numbers; the owner can see behaviour in the viewer once it exists. Odd behaviour he notices is high-value input. Ask him what he's seen.
- **Some checks only run on GitHub.** The full type check of the harness, tests, and benchmark needs the `@types/node` package, which cloud sessions may not be able to install. The simulation and analysis code can be type-checked locally; the rest is checked by the GitHub workflow. For Phase 0 that workflow passed every step (tests and both type checks) on pull request #1.
