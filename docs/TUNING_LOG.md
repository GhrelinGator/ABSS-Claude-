# Tuning log

Append-only. Every parameter or mechanism change made for tuning reasons goes here **before** the code changes (`GUARDRAILS.md` §4, step 6). If you're about to change a number and there's no entry here, stop.

## Entry format

```
### T-NNN: short title
- Date / session model:
- Symptom: what was observed (with run IDs)
- Causal chain + broken link: (DESIGN.md §7.x, which link, how it was measured)
- Classification: (a) bug / (b) ecological miscalibration / (c) missing mechanism / (d) genuine negative result / viability
- Change: exact parameter or mechanism, old → new
- Alibi or plausibility target: (for b: the real-world target and its source; for c: the alibi; for viability: why founders were dying)
- Owner approval: (required for c and ambiguous cases; link or quote)
- Null-model re-run: did the ablated run stay quiet? (if not, revert)
```

### T-002: Climate corrections (two)
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-15, Opus 5.5.
- **Process note:** both were found by the first statistics run and fixed before any test was committed; logged at the end of the session, not before each change.
- **1. Variability knob raised average rain.** Symptom: over 30 years, mean rain was 2.6, 3.75, and 5.0 mm per day at variability 0, 1, and 2. Cause: the wetness anomaly entered as exp(anomaly) on both the chance and the amount of rain, and the average of exp(x) grows with x's spread. Classification: **(a) bug in the design of the manipulation**, since E1 must vary spread without changing the mean. Change: the anomaly scales only the amount, as exp(anomaly − spread²/2), with spread raised 0.35 → 0.45 so droughts are still felt. Check: the average stays within 20% across settings (tested); the spread of season-mean rain grows (tested).
- **2. Storms soaked the desert.** Symptom: desert soil moisture was about half that of wet land; real deserts are far drier. Cause: storm rain was added at full strength regardless of local climate. Classification: **(b) ecological miscalibration.** Change: storm rain is scaled by the cell's mean rainfall, like ordinary rain. Target: desert soils well under half as moist as temperate ones (tested).
- **Social metrics involved:** none.

### T-005: Fire: corrections while building Phase 1.8
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-17, Opus 5.5 (unattended run). Entries written before each change this time.
- **Social metrics involved:** none. No agents exist. Targets are ecological: lightning-fire frequency and burned area per area and per real day in temperate climates, fire going out in rain, wood not smouldering for weeks.
- **Rounds** (symptom → classification → change):
  1. No fire ever started in 10 compressed years on a 192-cell map. Poles counted the storm's own rain as soaking them, so a strike never found dry wood, and rain put out smouldering wood every tick of the day, counting the whole day's rain each tick. **(a) bug** (big wood responds to rain over weeks; rain puts fire out only while it falls). Poles now follow soil moisture alone; rain-out uses the rain falling at that moment (a storm's rate under a storm, a wet day's rain spread over 24 hours); a strike's pole dryness reaches 0 at 45% moisture (was 30%).
  2. Then fires ran away: once lit in a drought, 56–81% of the land burned in a compressed year, 20,000–29,000 cells burned at once, and each cell flamed about 12 times. Two causes: smouldering lasted for weeks (3% of the poles an hour, never going out by itself), and burnt cells re-flamed because 30% of the remaining sticks counted as fine fuel. **(a) bug** for the re-burning (twigs burn in the front; what is left is not fine fuel); **(b)** for the smouldering (real embers in a log pile go out within hours to a few days). Fine fuel is now the litter alone; smouldering also goes out by itself at 6% an hour (half-life about 11 hours).
  3. Then 38 holdovers in 15 years, none ever flamed: curing came only from warmth and soil moisture, so green, moist summer grass never cured, though temperate grass cures in late summer after seeding whatever the rain. **(b) ecological miscalibration** (curing ignored phenology). During the dying-back season (season fraction 0.45–0.7, when the litter falls) plants keep only 30% of their greenness.
  4. On the full default page world (seed 7, hemmed in 0.75), 20 compressed years gave two fire events: one burned 52% of the land in a year, the other 12%, and nothing burned in the other 18 years. Burned area averaged about 3% of the land per compressed year, far above lightning-only fire regimes (roughly 0.1–1% of the land per *real* year, so about 0.01–0.15% per compressed year), and the sizes were all-or-nothing instead of mostly small. The diagnosis: estimated night humidity peaked near 80%, so fine fuel stayed near 14–17% moisture all night and fires kept spreading round the clock for weeks. **(b) ecological miscalibration** (the humidity proxy against temperate climatology: afternoons about 45–60%, nights 85–95% in summer, drier in droughts). The humidity estimate becomes 50% + 30% × soil moisture, +15% on a wet day, ±20% through the day (was 40% + 40% × soil, ±15%). Fine fuel under trees is also shaded: +5 points of moisture × tree cover (shaded-fuel corrections in fire-danger rating are a few points).
  5. The humidity change didn't alter the year-8 fire (still 52%). Traced hour by hour: fires did lie down at night and stop on wet days, but up to 26,000 cells (260 km²) flamed at once. Every cell flamed for one cell width ÷ the *backing* rate (up to 4 hours) even when a head fire crossed it in four minutes, so the flaming zone behind the front was kilometres deep and spread sideways and backwards almost surely. **(a) bug** in the cell model: a cell now flames for one cell width ÷ the rate at which the fire entered it (head-entered cells burn briefly and mostly push on; backing-entered ones burn long), within the same 10-minute to 4-hour limits; a fire starting at a point uses the backing rate.
  6. Still about 50% in that year, and the breakdown by mean rainfall showed it burned the *wettest* land (92% of land with rainfall above 1.2×, 65% of 0.8–1.2×, 1% of land below 0.5×). Real humid temperate pasture stays partly green through autumn (rarely over about 70% cured outside droughts), so lightning fire there is rare, while semi-arid grassland cures fully and burns often. The dying-back factor of round 3 cured moist and dry ground alike. **(b) ecological miscalibration.** Plants dying back keep 60% of their greenness (was 30%): on moist, warm ground that leaves the fuel 40% cured, too green to carry fire, until the soil dries or the cold comes; on dry ground it is cured either way.
     Result: no change (50.6%). The event is a drought fire: the valley's soil had dried, so the fuel was cured whatever the season factor. The 60% stays, as the better estimate for humid pasture, but it was not the cause.
  7. The shape was the cause of the size. My first directional rule, back + (head − back) × ((1 + cos θ)/2)², sent fire to the forward diagonal neighbours at 73% of the head rate and sideways at 25%, so fires fanned out about as wide as they were long. Real wind-driven fires grow as ellipses: at a 20 km/h wind the length is about 2.5 times the breadth, and the spread rate 45° off the wind is a small fraction of the head rate. **(a) bug** (not the standard model). Spread now follows the elliptical model with the fire at the rear focus: *R*(θ) = head × (1 − *e*) / (1 − *e* cos θ), never below the no-wind rate, with eccentricity *e* from the length-to-breadth ratio *LB* = 1 + 8.729 (1 − e^(−0.030 *U*))^2.155 (Alexander 1985, as used in the Canadian Fire Behaviour Prediction System; *U* = 10 m wind in km/h).
     Result: no change (51%). Traced day by day, the year was not a drought at all (wetness anomaly +0.25, mean soil moisture 0.6–0.77), yet 4–10% of the land burned on each dry day, mostly forest in midsummer.
  8. Curing counted trees and bushes at half weight, so a leafy forest floor in midsummer counted as about 60% cured. Under a closed summer canopy the floor is shaded and the understory green, which is why temperate broadleaf forest rarely burns in summer. **(b) ecological miscalibration.** Every living plant's cover now counts fully as green while it grows (live cover = total cover, at most 1).
     Result: no fire in 20 years on that map. On four 256-cell maps over 30 years (default; walled and droughty; open and droughty; variability 1.5): one fire event every 10–15 years per map, each burning 15–100% of the land. Still all-or-nothing.
  9. Two faults in the traces. Fire crossed one-cell rivers by stepping diagonally between two river cells, so rivers were never firebreaks. **(a) bug.** A diagonal step is now blocked when either cell it passes between is water. And smouldering survived four wet days in a row (3.9–11 mm each) because a wet day's rain was spread over 24 hours as a drizzle of under 0.5 mm an hour. **(a) bug** in how I used daily rain. A wet day now puts out smouldering cells with chance 10% per mm on the cell over the day (at most 95%), spread over its ticks; a storm's rain still counts by its rate. The same for burning objects.
  10. Writing the unit tests showed the spread model itself was wrong. On a uniform bed at a 23 km/h wind the fire backed into the wind at about 10 m/min and widened at about 25 m/min, against 2.3 and 13 m/min from its own rates. Each burning cell re-rolled its chance at each neighbour every sub-step, and a front of many cells takes the luckiest roll (a minimum of many exponential waiting times), so fronts outran their rate, most of all where the rate is slow. **(a) bug** in the method. Spread is now by minimum travel time (Finney 2002): a neighbour starts burning exactly when the fire, travelling from a burning cell at the rate toward it, reaches it, found in time order within each tick. Each cell gets a random vigour (0.7–1.3, drawn when it starts flaming) that scales the rates out of it, so edges stay irregular. A cell flames until the fire has left it in every direction (1.5 cell widths ÷ its slowest rate, 10 minutes to 4 hours).
  11. After round 10: stop calibrating here. Whatever remains goes to the ecology review (1.10 [F]) with this log, rather than more rounds in an unattended run.

### T-006: Ecology review (1.10): fire on open country
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-18, Opus 5.5, on an [F] milestone at the owner's request (no Fable credits). Entries written before each change.
- **Social metrics involved:** none. No agents exist.
- **Found by:** the preregistered Phase 1 exit runs (`experiments/P1-ecology-review/`). On open country (circumscription 0), fire was all-or-nothing: almost nothing burned for 30–40 compressed years, then a single fire year burned 70–143% of the land (more than 100% means cells burned twice in one year), with 16,000–85,000 flare-ups from smouldering wood. Mean burned share 1.1–2.1% of land per compressed year against the preregistered ceiling of 0.5%. The fire milestone (T-005) had been calibrated on the hemmed-in page world, where fire stays small.
- **Rounds** (symptom → classification → change):
  1. Cells burned twice within weeks. Litter falls in the dying-back season (season 0.45–0.7) in proportion to plant cover, and a fire top-kills only 15% of grass cover, so a meadow burned in late summer received almost its whole year's litter afterwards, and smouldering wood relit it. A fire burns the year's dead grass and herbs standing; that material cannot also fall later. **(a) bug.** In a cell that has burned since the start of the current year, only woody plants (berry bushes, nut trees) add litter for the rest of that year.
  2. In hemmed-in worlds (circumscription 1) deer collapsed: 5 per km² of land fell to 3.3 by year 5 after 4,000–5,300 hunger deaths in year 3, then declined to 0.02 by year 100, and in seed 2 wolves and big cats died out after them. Starting numbers were densities per km² of *all* dry land, deserts included, but animals were placed only where food grows; a hemmed map is about one-third habitable, so the valley started at roughly three times the intended density and starved. **(a) setup bug.** Starting numbers are now densities per km² of *habitat*: land counts in proportion to its forage, fully at 100 kg per hectare or more. Hares start in proportion to forage against the same yardstick rather than the map's mean.
  3. After the crash, deer births stayed near 5% of the herd a year (45% when well fed), so the herd was underfed while predators, held up by hares and aurochs, kept killing about 200 a year. Deer searched only 800 m for better food when theirs ran short, so groups crowded the same patches and stripped them while other parts of the valley went unused (the tight clusters noted in `STATUS.md`). **(b) ecological miscalibration** (a missing behaviour: animals range farther when hungry). A grazer below 70% condition compares spots up to 3 km away instead of 800 m.
  - **Results:** round 1 stopped double burns from refuelled meadows (worst year 174% → 105%; the 105% is nut-tree leaf litter refuelling woodland burned earlier the same year, which is physical) but not the all-or-nothing regime (mean 1.15% of land per compressed year on 8 worlds). Rounds 2 and 3 fixed the hemmed collapses: in the follow-up all 8 worlds keep every kind, deer hold or grow in every hemmed valley, and nothing exceeds 5× its start. Fire goes on to milestone 1.11.

### T-007: Fire regime on open country (milestone 1.11)
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-19, Opus 5.5 (unattended run). Entries written before each change.
- **Social metrics involved:** none. No agents exist. Targets (`DESIGN.md` §2.16, end): open country 0.4–0.9% of land burned per compressed year, no year above 25%; hemmed valleys at or below 0.5%.
- **Diagnosis before any change** (`experiments/P1-fire-regime/diagnose.ts`, open seeds 1–4 on 256-cell maps, 100 compressed years; output in `experiments/P1-fire-regime/results.md`):
  - Of 150–165 lightning holdovers per world, only 3–4 ever flamed. Every lightning start was a holdover: the storm's rain already wets the fine fuel it lands on (lever 1 is in effect already), so ignitions are not the problem; the size of the few fires that get going is.
  - The fires that flamed started in the dying-back season (season 0.46–0.64), mostly on soil that was *not* in drought (map soil moisture 0.43–0.93), and burned 6–30 days, through 3–36 nights with no flame anywhere but smouldering cells left, and through 4–10 wet days and 2–3 storms. Each burned 25–113% of the map (counting re-ignitions; 24–99% distinct).
  - A third of all cell ignitions came between 19:00 and 07:00. A day-by-day trace of open seed 4's year-44 fire: fine fuel at the burning edge stood at 21–23% moisture at 03:00, just under the 24% at which it stops carrying flame, so the front crept on all night for 14 dry nights; 300–8,900 cells smouldered at dawn, and 100–300 of them flared each day.
  - Open country is a grassy woodland: 96% of its land has 10–40% tree cover, 3.2–4.3 t/ha of litter, and 4–7.5 t/ha of fallen wood, so every cell can smoulder.
  - Grazing removes about 1% of the year's grass litter on average (aurochs and hares eat about 8 kg/ha in a 48-day compressed year against about 900 kg/ha of grass litter), so lever 3 (litter from uneaten grass only) could not change the regime except at a few heavily grazed spots. Not tried.
- **Rounds** (symptom → classification → change):
  1. Fronts never stop at night on ground that is not in drought. The fuel moisture model has vapour exchange only (Simard's equilibrium moisture tops out at about 27% at saturation), and no condensation, but on humid nights dew forms on grass and wets dead fine fuel well above the moisture at which grass fire stops (Viney 1991 reviews condensation as one of the two liquid-water inputs to fine fuel moisture). The standard empirical rule for when surfaces are wet with dew is air humidity at or above 90% (Sentelhas et al. 2008, *Agricultural and Forest Meteorology* 148: 392–400: the RH ≥ 90% rule estimated leaf wetness best of the models tested). **(b) ecological miscalibration** (night fuel moisture too low on humid nights; this is lever 2 in `DESIGN.md` §2.16). Change: fine fuel gains a dew term of up to 15 points of moisture, rising from none at an estimated humidity of 88% to all of it at 92%, times the open sky above it (1 − tree cover; a canopy keeps the ground from cooling enough to collect dew). It burns off by itself as the morning humidity falls below the threshold. Old: no dew.
     Result (open seeds 1–4, 256 cells, 100 years): mean 1.37% of land per compressed year (was 1.45%), worst years 18–103% (were 1.5–113%), a quarter of ignitions still at night. No real change: with the humidity estimate at 0.7 + 0.3 × soil moisture before dawn, dew forms only on soil wetter than about 0.6, and these fires burn on soil of 0.45–0.65. Kept, as the better physics, like T-005 round 6; not the cause.
  2. Why fronts creep on through 21–23% fuel moisture at night. Cheney et al. (1998)'s moisture function has two linear forms above 12% moisture: 0.684 − 0.0342 *M* when the 10 m wind is under 10 km/h, and 0.547 − 0.0228 *M* at 10 km/h or more (Eq. 3.7 in Cruz et al. 2015, *A guide to rate of fire spread models for Australian vegetation*, CSIRO and AFAC). So a grass fire stops at 20% fuel moisture in light wind and at 24% only in a stronger one. The code used the strong-wind form at every wind speed, and night winds here are mostly under 10 km/h (the daily swing takes 40% off the mean of about 12.6 km/h). **(a) bug** (half of a source equation implemented). Change: the moisture factor takes the wind reaching the fire and uses the light-wind form below 10 km/h (extinction at 20%), the strong-wind form above. Nothing else changes; above 10 km/h every number is as before.
     Result (open seeds 1–8, 256 cells, 100 years): mean 1.12% of land per compressed year, worst years 5–104%; night ignitions down to 12–35% of the total. Fires that get going are still huge. A day-by-day trace of open seed 5's year-28 fire (wetness +1.05, a wet year) showed why: it burned for 24 days on saturated soil (0.93–1.00), through rains of 31.5, 22.5, 26.2, 5.2 and 10.3 mm. After each, a few of its 600–3,500 smouldering cells survived, and the next dry afternoon the fine fuel stood at 11–12% moisture again, so the fire took off from them (9,470 cells in the day after a 22.5 mm rain).
  3. Fine fuel forgets rain at midnight: rain adds 6 points of moisture per mm for the rest of the day it falls and nothing after, so litter soaked by 30 mm is as dry as ever by the next afternoon. Real litter and fine dead fuel hold rain water for a day or more: the Canadian Fine Fuel Moisture Code, built for litter and fine fuels, has a nominal time lag of two-thirds of a day (Van Wagner 1987), so after a soaking it takes one to three drying days to carry fire again. **(b) ecological miscalibration** (the fine fuel's drying time after rain). Change: the water rain adds to the fine fuel carries over from the end of the day it fell, shrinking by e every 16 hours (two-thirds of a day). Ordinary rain on a wet day falls on the whole map, so it is kept as one running amount (times each cell's share of mean rainfall); a storm's rain is kept for each of the last few storms with its place and size. The same 6 points per mm; today's rain still counts in full all day, as before. Old: nothing carried past midnight.
     Result (open seeds 1–3 and 5–7; 4 and 8 not needed): almost no fire. One fire flamed in six worlds (seed 3, 18% of the map over 10 days); every other holdover went out first. Lightning comes only in storms, and the storm's 5–25 mm now keeps the litter around a strike too wet to carry flame until about midday two days later, while a holdover smoulders out at 6% an hour. Real lightning fires mostly show themselves within a day of the strike: in a global database of holdover times, more than half of the fires in 30 of 42 data sets were found within 24 hours, and medians run from 1.4 to 53.6 hours (Moris et al. 2023, *Earth System Science Data* 15: 1151). So real strikes often land on fuel that was not soaked. Kept (the drying time is right; see round 4 for what was wrong with the storms).
  4. A storm's rain falls evenly over its whole circle, and every strike lands inside that circle, so every strike lands in the storm's full rain. Real thunderstorm rain is concentrated under the storm's core and thins toward its edges, and lightning reaches the light-rain margins and beyond ("lightning often strikes outside of heavy rain and may occur as far as 10 miles from any rainfall", US National Weather Service lightning-safety guidance). **(b) ecological miscalibration** (the shape of a storm's rain; this is lever 1 in `DESIGN.md` §2.16 from the other side: a strike should be wetted by the rain that actually falls where it lands). Change: a storm's rain depth falls from twice its mean at the centre to nothing at its edge, 2 × mean × (1 − (d/R)²), the same total water as before. Soil moisture, the fuel's wetting, the storm's rain putting out smouldering wood, and the rain carried over (round 3) all use the same shape. Strikes stay where they were. Old: the mean depth everywhere inside the circle.
     Result (open seeds 1–3 and 5–7): fires now start in flame at the storm's light-rain edge, but only five flamed in six worlds, the largest 100 cells (1 km²). Mean burned share 0.000–0.002% of land per compressed year, far under the band. Kept.
  5. Lightning holdovers go out too soon. They use the same 6%-an-hour self-extinction as the embers a front leaves in sticks and logs (T-005 round 2, "embers in a log pile go out within hours to a few days"), a half-life of about 11 hours, so almost none outlast the soaked litter around them. A strike's smouldering starts at the base of the struck tree, in its roots and the organic matter around them, where it is sheltered and slow (Moris et al. 2023 name the organic layer at the base of struck trees as a usual ignition point); real lightning holdover times run from hours to several days, with medians up to 53.6 hours across data sets, occasionally weeks. **(b) ecological miscalibration** (holdover survival against holdover-time data). Change: a cell set smouldering by lightning goes out by itself at 1.5% an hour (half-life about 46 hours, two days) until it flares or a front reaches it; everything else about it is unchanged (rain still puts it out as before). Smouldering left behind a front keeps 6% an hour. Old: 6% an hour for both.
     Result (open seeds 1–8): fires start again, 2–13 per world, about 40% of them staying under 1 km², but the ones that get going still burn 80–110% of the map. Mean 2.75% of land per compressed year (1.45% before round 1), worst years 82–110%. Hemmed valleys (seeds 1–4): mean 0.21%, worst year 18%, inside their band; the valley walls stop the fires.
  - **Stopped here** (classification of what remains: (c) missing mechanism). Every round was physically right on its own terms and none limits a fire's size once it is going: a front of 1,000–4,000 cells, each with tonnes of smouldering wood, comes back from a night, a dew or a light rain if one cell in a few hundred keeps an ember, and only rain heavy enough to keep the litter wet for several days ends it. Real fires are held to local and landscape sizes by breaks in the fuel, which open country here lacks (96% of its land carries the same 3–4 t/ha of litter edge to edge). The design already lists the two missing pieces, sub-cell patchiness of fuel and moist ground along streams (`DESIGN.md` §2.16, *Not in this version*). Adding either is a new mechanism and needs the owner's approval (`GUARDRAILS.md` §4, D-025, D-033). The rounds stay in: the open regime now has too many large fires rather than a few, and the next step is the same either way. Details: `experiments/P1-fire-regime/results.md`.

### T-008: Breaks in the fuel, then fire on open country to the band (milestone 1.12)
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-19, Opus 5.5 (unattended run, an [F] milestone on Opus, D-034). Entries written before each change.
- **Social metrics involved:** none. No agents exist. Targets as in T-007 (`DESIGN.md` §2.16, end) and the preregistered Phase 1 check (`experiments/P1-ecology-review/preregistration.md`): mean burned share across all 20 worlds at most 0.5% of land per compressed year, no world above 25% in any compressed year.
- **The two new mechanisms** (the owner's yes, D-034; design in `DESIGN.md` §2.10 steps 11–12, §2.11, §2.16 *Breaks in the fuel*): a water table level with the river, lake, spring or small stream (0.5 km² of catchment) a cell drains to, which tops the soil up from below (6 mm a day at the surface, e-fold 0.6 m); and bare ground by soil, which fire must bridge (continuum percolation of fuel patches, flames reaching half their Byram length). They are mechanisms, not tuning; their numbers are estimates written into the design before any fire run.
- **What they did** (full-size open maps, 100 compressed years, `experiments/P1-fire-regime/diagnose.ts`): with the water table alone, open seed 1: mean 1.40% of land per compressed year, worst year 57%, 15 fires (8 under 1 km²); open seed 2: 1.21%, worst 93%, 10 fires (5 under 1 km²). Fires now come in many sizes (largest per world: 54,985, 134,243, 142,083 cells; 35,221, 31,502, 232,556), but drought-year fires still cross most of the map, burning 10–33 days through 5–25 nights and 6–11 wet days. Damp ground forms a branching network along streams (about a quarter of the land within 2 m of its water table), and a branching network never closes round anything: a fire walks round the heads of the streams. Bare ground hardly changes open country, which is 95% loam (3% bare); it matters on the walls of hemmed valleys.
- **The preregistered check, re-run** (20 full-size worlds; `experiments/P1-ecology-review/results.md`, last section): fire mean 0.64% of land per compressed year across all worlds (limit 0.5%; 1.24% in the 1.10 run), open 0.95%, hemmed 0.32%; worst year 87% (limit 25%; was 174%); seven of twenty worlds have a year above 25%. Almost all burning is in autumn and early winter (season 0.5–0.875). Persistence failed on open seed 6, whose deer starved beside uneaten greens and then lost their predators; the same world on `main` kept them but already starved hundreds of deer a year.
- **No calibration round was made.** The levers that remain are not a number to move toward a target: the damp network cannot enclose a fire whatever its width, and loam carries fire whatever its small share of bare ground. What would bound fires on open country is a further mechanism or a judgment about the target (`STATUS.md` Q10). Classification of what remains: **ambiguous** between (c) missing mechanism (a patchier woodland, or grazers that thin the grass as much as real ones do; the compressed year makes them eat about 1% of it, T-007) and (d) a genuine property of an unbroken grassy woodland with no people. So it goes to the owner (`GUARDRAILS.md` §4, step 7).

### T-009: Open seed 6's starving deer (milestone 1.13)
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-19, Opus 5.5 (unattended run, an [F] milestone on Opus, D-034). Entries written before each change.
- **Social metrics involved:** none. No agents exist. Target: the preregistered persistence criterion (`experiments/P1-ecology-review/preregistration.md`); the yardstick is the other open worlds, where deer starve 0–100 a year.
- **Diagnosis before any change** (`experiments/P1-open-seed-6/diagnose.ts` and `follow.ts`, full-size open seed 6 against open seed 1, 35 compressed years; output in `experiments/P1-open-seed-6/results.md`):
  - Food is not short on open seed 6. Yet seed 6 starves 550–1,000 deer a year from year 18.
  - Every starving deer is in one place. At year 20 all 26 starving groups (230 deer), and every deer that had gone more than 100 hours without drinking (409), stand on one row of cells at 32.65 km north, between 49.95 km east and the map's east edge (51.2 km). Hour by hour they shuttle between the two ends: thirsty at the edge, they walk the shortest way toward water, a stretch of 12 cells at a time, which ends exactly at the centre of cell (499, 326); a wolf pack and a big cat sit at exactly that point, at a carcass; at distance zero `flee()` has no direction away, so it uses its fallback, due east, and the deer run 1.5 km east into the map edge, where they stop; an hour later the walk to water brings them back to exactly the same point. They never reach water, never search for food (thirst and flight come first), and eat the row bare. The predators never leave, because the deer starving beside them keep feeding them. The deer that drift into the row stay until they die: hundreds a year for decades.

  1. A predator at exactly the deer's position sends them due east, every time. **(a) bug** (a fixed fallback direction in a degenerate case; a real animal bolts some way, and which way is not fixed by the compass). Change: in `flee()` and `leaveFire()`, at distance zero, the direction is drawn at random from the ecology stream, uniform over the circle. Old: due east.
     Result: reloading the year-20 moment of open seed 6 with the fix, every trapped group reached water within a day, and the starving deer fell from 230 to 68 in 12 days as they fed. Run from the start (35 compressed years), seed 6's hunger deaths fell from 490–980 a year (years 18–34) to 0–56, and its herd grew like the other open worlds' (32,000 deer at year 34, against 31,600 on seed 1 and 29,800 on seed 3). In the full exit check (`experiments/P1-ecology-review/results.md`, last section) open seed 6 ends at 11.4 deer per km² (minimum 5.0), with its wolves and big cats.
  - **Not changed:** the map edge still stops a flight dead, and animals don't die of thirst (both part of §2.13's simple design). A pack within 1.5 km of the edge, on a route to water, could still hold deer in a shorter loop; none was seen.

### T-010: Wound infections that could never kill (milestone 2.3)
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-19, Opus 5.5 (unattended run, [O] milestone). Written before the change.
- **Social metrics involved:** none. No agents exist in real runs. Target: §3.10's calibration target, written before the first run: of wound infections in fed adults with water, 2–15% kill; in adults kept at about 3% body fat, at least twice as many (`tests/unit/injury.test.ts`).
- **Symptom (first run of the tests):** 300 fed and 300 thin bodies, each with a dirty 60 cm² wound: 295 and 299 infected, none died. Cleared courses lasted a median 19.5 days (target 1–4 weeks, met).
- **Cause:** the course was written as *ds*/*dt* = *g s* (1 − *s*) − *m s* for both routes, with death at 0.9. The logistic factor (1 − *s*) slows growth as severity nears 1, so the response always catches up before 0.9: run alone, a strain three times the median virulence peaks at 0.79 in a fed body and 0.88 in a thin one. The design's own estimate of how many die (about 10% and 25%) had assumed growth without that factor. **(a) bug**, in the design's arithmetic: the factor belongs to the gut, whose lining is finite (it bounds diarrhoea), not to an infection spreading through the body, which is not bounded until it kills.
- **Change:** wound infections grow as *ds*/*dt* = *g s* − *m s* and kill at a severity of 1; the gut keeps the logistic factor and kills only through the water it takes (§3.10 updated). No number changed. Run alone, the critical virulence is then 0.96 a day in a fed body (9.6% of strains above it) and 0.70 in a body at 3% fat (25%), with deaths around day 5–8.

### T-011: Growth that lagged its curve (milestone 2.5)
- **Date / session model:** 2026-11-19, Opus 5.5 (unattended run, [O] milestone). Written before the change.
- **Social metrics involved:** none. No agents exist in real runs. Target: §3.12's calibration target, written before the first run: a newborn kept fed grows within 5% of its frame curve at 2, 5, 10, 15 and 20 (`tests/unit/aging.test.ts`). The curve itself (from Walker et al. 2006) was not touched.
- **Symptom (first run of the tests):** with the growth time constant at 10 days, a fed newborn passed at 2 (4.5% behind) but was 18% behind its curve at 3 months and 13% at a year. A first-order lag trails a rising target by its slope × the time constant, and the compressed year makes infancy steep: the frame almost triples in the first compressed year (48 real days).
- **Classification:** (b) **miscalibration** of the estimate's own stated purpose ("short enough that a fed child stays within about 1% of the curve"), not of any outcome: the time constant stands for how closely a child with food to spare follows its growth programme, which in real children is closely.
- **Change:** `LIFECOURSE.growthDays` 10 → 2. A fed newborn is now 5% behind at 3 months, 2.7% at a year and within 1% from 2 on; it reaches its adult frame at 25. Catch-up after a shortage is correspondingly quick while the body is young enough to grow (below 25).
